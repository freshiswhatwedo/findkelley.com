Keywave — PGP encryption for iPhone

Keywave is a free PGP encryption app for iPhone, built by William Kelley. It encrypts and decrypts messages and manages your keys entirely on your device. No servers, no account, no tracking. It needs iOS 17.6 or later and uses ProtonMail's open-source gopenpgp library for the cryptography.

Your messages. Your keys. Nobody else's business.

Released 9 March 2026 · Updated 2 November 2026

What does Keywave do?

Keywave is a PGP tool, not a messaging app. You choose your keys, type or paste a message, and tap encrypt or decrypt. Copy the result and send it however you like. Keywave generates 2048, 3072 or 4096-bit RSA keypairs with optional password protection, stores as many keys as you want, and lets you search them by name, email or fingerprint. You can import ASCII-armored keys from files (.asc, .gpg, .key, .pub, .pem), from the clipboard, or by sharing key text into Keywave from another app, and export them again as ASCII-armored or binary. Everything runs locally on the phone using ProtonMail's open-source gopenpgp library. Keywave has no server to talk to, so there is nothing for it to send your data to. It is free with every feature included. No ads. No in-app purchases.

  1. Encrypt and decrypt. Swipe between modes. Select keys. Tap. Copy.
  2. Generate keys. 2048, 3072 or 4096-bit RSA. Optional password.
  3. Manage keys. Search, filter, sort. Public, private, expiring, expired.
  4. Import. Share sheet from any app, Files, or the clipboard.
  5. Export. ASCII-armored or binary.
  6. Local. No servers. No account. No analytics.

Does Keywave need an account or a server?

No. Keywave has no accounts and no server. Encryption, decryption and key storage happen on your iPhone, and the app makes no network requests of its own.

How do I get a key into Keywave?

Four ways. Share it in from another app: select the key text anywhere — Telegram, Mail, Notes — tap Share, and pick Keywave. Open a key file from the Files app and choose Keywave. Paste it from the clipboard. Or generate your own keypair in the app, 2048, 3072 or 4096-bit RSA, with an optional password.

Is Keywave open source?

The cryptography is: Keywave is built on ProtonMail's open-source gopenpgp library. Keywave's own source code is not published.

Where are my keys stored?

Your keys themselves are held in the iOS Keychain on your iPhone, marked device-only, so they are not synced to iCloud and do not travel in a backup to another device. The details you see in the key list — name, email, fingerprint — are stored separately on the device and encrypted. Nothing is sent anywhere. Back your keys up by exporting them yourself.

Is it safe to use with real keys?

Keywave has not had an independent security audit. The cryptography comes from gopenpgp, which is widely used and publicly auditable; Keywave's job is to call it correctly and keep keys on the device.

How do I import a key?

Open an ASCII-armored key file (.asc, .gpg, .key, .pub or .pem) from the Files app and choose Keywave, paste the key from the clipboard, or share key text into Keywave from another app.

How do I back up my keys?

Export them ASCII-armored and save to Files or share. Keywave has no cloud, so there is no cloud backup. If you delete the app without exporting, the keys are gone.

Can I use my existing GPG keys?

Yes, if you export them ASCII-armored — gpg --armor --export, or --export-secret-keys for a private key. Keywave reads armored keys; it does not read binary keyring files.

Does Keywave collect anything?

No data is collected. See the privacy policy.
